Privacy

Edwell is built to be trusted with your footage, so it asks nothing of you and sends nothing about you.

The app

Edwell has no account and no tracking, and it shows no ads. It doesn't measure how you use it: the only figures it sends are the crash reports and release-health notes below. Editing and export happen on your Mac. Edwell reads the clips, photos and music you add to a project, and it writes only the projects and videos you save, where you choose. It never sends your footage or your projects anywhere.

Crash reports

When Edwell crashes or hangs, it sends a crash report so I can find out what went wrong and fix it. A report says where in Edwell's code the problem happened, and it carries the version of Edwell and of macOS, the Mac's model and chip, how much memory it had, and its language, region and time zone settings. Edwell also sends a short release-health note when you start and stop using it, so I can tell whether a new version crashes more often than the last one. A note carries Edwell's version, when the session started and how long it lasted, how many errors it had and whether it ended in a crash, and a random number of its own so its start and end can be matched. And it passes on the reports macOS makes for apps about hangs and heavy use of the processor or the disk. On the App Store's privacy label all of this is Data Not Linked to You, under Diagnostics: Crash Data, Performance Data and Other Diagnostic Data.

The reports carry no ID. Every Mac sends them as the same anonymous user, so a report can't be tied to you or to your Mac. They never include your footage, your projects or your titles, and before a report leaves your Mac, Edwell takes out the file paths and file names it can recognise. None of it is linked to you or used for tracking. To do this, Edwell keeps the current session's note, any reports waiting to be sent and the shared anonymous name on your Mac, and nothing that identifies you.

The reports go to Sentry, a crash-reporting service, and are stored in the EU. Sentry is set not to store IP addresses. You can read Sentry's privacy policy. If you'd rather send nothing, turn off Send Anonymous Crash Reports in the Help menu. Edwell then sends one last note to close the current session, and nothing after that. Edwell 1.1 and earlier send no reports at all.

The trial and the purchase

The 14-day trial and the purchase go through Apple's App Store. Apple takes care of the payment, and Edwell never sees your payment details. All it learns from the App Store is the price, when your trial started and whether you've bought Edwell, so it knows whether export is open. Apart from the crash reports, the App Store is the only thing Edwell connects to.

Send Feedback

Send Feedback in the Help menu opens a new email in your own mail app, addressed to support@iamjarl.com. It fills in the version of Edwell, the version of macOS and whether your Mac has Apple silicon or Intel. You can read and change all of it, and nothing is sent unless you send it yourself.

The website

This site uses privacy-friendly, cookieless analytics (Umami) to count visits. It records no personal data and sets no cookies.

Questions

If you have any questions about privacy in Edwell, you can email support@iamjarl.com.
